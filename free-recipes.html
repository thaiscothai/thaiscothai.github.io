Chicken Satay with Peanut Sauce

Golden grilled chicken skewers with a rich, creamy peanut dipping sauce

Serves3–4
Prep25 mins + marinating
Cook12–15 mins
HeatMedium-High
Ingredients

Chicken Satay with Peanut Sauce

  • For the chicken satay
  • Chicken thigh or breast, cut into strips500g
  • Coconut milk4 tbsp
  • Yellow curry powder2 tbsp
  • Fish sauce1 tbsp
  • Palm sugar2 tbsp
  • Garlic, minced3 cloves
  • Ginger, grated1 tbsp
  • Turmeric½ tsp
  • Bamboo skewers, soakedas needed
  • For the peanut sauce
  • Peanut butter4 tbsp
  • Coconut milk3 tbsp
  • Light soy sauce1 tbsp
  • Dark soy sauce1 tsp
  • Palm sugar2 tbsp
  • Lime juice1 tbsp
  • Crushed peanuts2 tbsp
Method
  1. Soak bamboo skewers in water for at least 30 minutes. Cut chicken into even strips.
  2. Combine coconut milk, curry powder, fish sauce, palm sugar, garlic, ginger and turmeric. Coat chicken thoroughly; refrigerate to marinate for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Thread chicken onto skewers. Grill or cook on a hot griddle over medium-high heat, turning regularly, until nicely charred and fully cooked. Chicken must reach 74°C (165°F) internally.
  4. For the sauce, gently warm peanut butter, coconut milk, soy sauces and palm sugar in a small pan. Stir until smooth, adding a little water if needed.
  5. Remove sauce from heat; stir in lime juice. Top with crushed peanuts and serve alongside the hot skewers.
Chef tip · Draft for Pirin’s review

For the best char, leave a little space between the pieces on each skewer. Keep the peanut sauce warm, not boiling.

Som Tum — Papaya Pok Pok Salad

Fresh green papaya, lime, chilli and peanuts, pounded together Isan-style

Serves2–3
Prep15–20 mins
CookNone
HeatNo cooking · Spicy
Ingredients

Som Tum — Papaya Pok Pok Salad

  • Green papaya, shredded300g
  • Cherry tomatoes, halved10–12
  • Long beans, cut into 3cm pieces4–5
  • Garlic2–3 cloves
  • Bird’s eye chillies3–5, to taste
  • Dried shrimp (optional)1 tbsp
  • Palm sugar1 tbsp
  • Fish sauce2 tbsp
  • Fresh lime juice2 tbsp
  • Roasted peanuts2 tbsp
Method
  1. Peel the green papaya and shred it into long, fine strips. Prepare the tomatoes and long beans.
  2. In a large mortar, gently pound garlic and chillies to release their flavour. Start with fewer chillies if you prefer a milder salad.
  3. Add long beans and lightly bruise them with the pestle. Add palm sugar, fish sauce and lime juice; stir and pound gently to dissolve the sugar.
  4. Add tomatoes and dried shrimp, if using. Lightly press them to release their juices without turning them to pulp.
  5. Add shredded papaya. Use a spoon and pestle together to toss and lightly bruise the ingredients until coated in dressing.
  6. Taste and adjust the balance of sour, salty, sweet and spicy. Finish with roasted peanuts and serve immediately.
Chef tip · Draft for Pirin’s review

Pok pok is the sound of the pestle. Bruise the papaya gently rather than crushing it — you want a lively crunch and a punchy dressing.
